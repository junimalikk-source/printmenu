The short version

What is Indian takeaway menu printing?

Indian takeaway menu printing is the design and print production of menus built specifically for UK curry houses, balti restaurants and Indian fast-food takeaways. Unlike generic menu printing, cuisine-specific Indian menu printing is built around the order Indian customers expect (starters and poppadoms, mains grouped by meat, biryani, breads and sides), with Bengali, Urdu or Hindi typesetting alongside the English description, and the halal certification and dietary labelling curry-house customers look for.

The standard specification across the UK trade is A4 double-sided, full colour CMYK, on 130gsm gloss paper, printed via offset litho for runs of 10,000 or more. Most UK Indian takeaways reprint every 4 to 6 months for door drop campaigns, with planned reprints around Eid, Ramadan and Diwali where those festivals drive trade.

The anatomy of an Indian menu

The six sections every Indian takeaway menu needs.

The proven structure of a high-converting UK Indian takeaway menu, in the order customers read it.

1

Starters and poppadoms

Onion bhajis, samosas, sheek kebab, chicken tikka, mixed platters. The top-left of the menu front. Always include the poppadom set offer here (poppadoms with a chutney tray) as the cheapest entry point and an anchor for the order total.

2

Mains grouped by meat

Chicken curries, lamb curries, king prawn, vegetarian. Within each meat group, list dishes from mild to hot (korma, masala, balti, jalfrezi, vindaloo, phaal). This is the largest section and takes 40 to 60 percent of menu space.

3

Biryani and signature dishes

Biryanis are typically a separate section because they include rice. House specials and chef's signatures sit here. A background tint or border helps these higher-margin dishes stand out on the page.

4

Breads, rice and sides

Naan, roti, paratha, peshwari, garlic naan, pilau rice, mushroom rice. Sides include bhajis, raitas and chutneys. Position bottom-right of the front or top of the back, where customers naturally add to their order.

5

Set meals and deals

Banquet for two, family deal, lunchtime special. UK Indian customers respond strongly to set deals priced 15 to 25 percent below their components. Position prominently on the back with a tinted background or border to draw the eye.

6

Desserts, drinks and contact

Kulfi, gulab jamun, kheer, mango lassi, soft drinks. Phone number, opening hours, delivery radius and online ordering platforms (Just Eat, Deliveroo, Uber Eats) sit at the foot. Halal certification and dietary symbols belong here too.

Compliance, handled

Halal, vegan and dietary labelling on Indian menus.

UK Indian takeaway customers expect clear dietary information. We label menus with halal certification logos (from HFA, HMC or your local authority), vegetarian (V), vegan (VG), gluten-free (GF) and dairy-free (DF) symbols. UK food information regulations require accurate allergen disclosure on every printed menu.

Send us your dietary symbols and we integrate them into the layout. We position halal certification prominently on the front of the menu where Muslim customers expect to verify it before ordering. Vegan and vegetarian symbols sit next to each qualifying dish, never grouped in a separate section unless the menu is large enough to warrant it.

Questions, answered

Indian takeaway menu printing FAQs

Common questions from UK Indian takeaway and curry house owners.

How much does Indian takeaway menu printing cost in the UK?

A4 Indian takeaway menus cost £425 for 10,000 copies, printed full colour CMYK on 130gsm gloss both sides. A5 menus start at £200 for 10,000. Free design, free Bengali and Urdu typesetting, and free UK delivery come included on every order.

Can you typeset Indian takeaway menus in Bengali, Urdu or Hindi?

Yes, we typeset Indian takeaway menus in English, Bengali, Urdu and Hindi every week. Send your dish names and restaurant name in your preferred script. We handle the layout, font selection, proofing and print-ready PDF with all scripts rendered correctly. No extra charge.

What size works best for Indian takeaway menus?

A4 double-sided is the standard for UK Indian takeaway menus. It fits 60 to 90 dishes across starters, mains by meat type, biryani, breads, sides and desserts without crowding. Curry houses with 100+ dishes use A4+ extended or A3. Compact menus under 40 dishes use A5.

How often should an Indian takeaway reprint its menu?

Most UK Indian takeaways reprint every 4 to 6 months for door drop campaigns. High-volume curry houses in Bradford, Birmingham, Leicester and East London reprint quarterly. Plan reprints around Eid, Ramadan and Diwali if those festivals drive your trade, allowing 2 weeks lead time.

Do you provide Indian food photography for menus?

Yes, we hold a 20-year stock library of Indian food photography covering curries, biryanis, tandoori platters, naan breads, samosas, pakoras and desserts. You can use our images free, supply your own, or book a custom photoshoot. We match the warm tones that sell Indian food.

How long does Indian takeaway menu printing take?

3 to 5 working days from artwork approval to your door. The full cycle takes 5 to 10 working days from first contact: 1 to 2 days for design, 1 to 2 days for proof revisions, then 3 to 5 days for offset litho printing and free UK delivery.

Do you print halal certification labels on Indian menus?

Yes, we display halal certification logos and dietary labels (vegan, vegetarian, gluten-free, dairy-free) clearly on Indian takeaway menus. Send your halal authority logo and dietary symbols and we integrate them into the layout. Most UK Indian takeaways prominently mark halal status.

Do I pay VAT on Indian takeaway menu printing?

No, printed Indian takeaway menus are zero-rated for VAT under HMRC's VAT Notice 701/10 on printed matter. The price you see is the price you pay, no VAT added. Always confirm with your accountant for your specific business setup.
